AP Physics 1 Free-Body Diagram Practice

Sketch a block on a ramp, check which forces act on it, and see what changes when it slides the other way.

Draw it before you calculate

A block slides down a rough ramp that rises to the right at 30°. Air resistance is negligible and nothing is pulling the block. Draw the free-body diagram for the block and choose axes. Which arrows belong, and which direction does each point?

  1. Sketch the ramp and mark the block’s direction of motion off to the side.
  2. Draw a separate dot for the block and label each force arrow by what exerts it.
  3. Choose positive x downhill and positive y away from the surface. Write the net force along each axis in terms of the block’s mass, gravity, the normal force, and kinetic friction.
Get one hint

Make a list of the objects interacting with the block. The ramp exerts two forces. The arrow showing how the block is moving belongs on your sketch of the ramp.

Check your diagram

Show the worked answer

Gravity points vertically down. The normal force points away from the ramp, perpendicular to its surface. Kinetic friction points up the ramp, opposing the block’s sliding relative to the ramp.

With the axes above, the forces sum to mg sin(30°) − fk along x and N − mg cos(30°) along y. The y sum is zero while the block remains in contact with the straight ramp. Draw the components of gravity on a separate sketch or label them clearly as components of the same arrow.

Retry. The block is now sliding up the ramp after a brief push, and the hand has lost contact. Redraw only the arrows that change.

Check the retry

Friction now points down the ramp. Weight and the normal force keep their directions. With positive x still downhill, both the downhill component of gravity and friction contribute positively to the x sum.

Ask for feedback on one arrow
